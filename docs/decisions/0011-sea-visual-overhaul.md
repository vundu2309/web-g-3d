# 0011: The sea's visual overhaul (water, islands, people, camera)

Status: accepted (user-directed, 2026-11-13); steps 1 to 4 below are on `main`, reflections and bushes were in progress when this was written.

## Context

The user found the sea flat and repetitive (a grid of ripples, one flat colour, no surf), the islands too pointy to look real, the market people ugly, and the camera too low and close. They asked for the water first, then the rest, with these standing rules: **make it beautiful first, phone performance is refactored later**, the sunset stays fixed, and every visual change is shown as before/after images (software-rendered, never a real phone) and approved before it is pushed. The images are the evidence; nothing here has been run on a real phone and no frame rate has been measured.

## Decisions

1. **Waves and ripples** (`waves.ts`, `ripples.ts`): twelve waves (2.2 to 61 m) spread around `WIND_DIRECTION`; fine ripples from a 128 x 128 slope texture built at start-up and read in two layers, modulated by calm and rough patches and faint wind streaks. The CPU height the boats float on is the same table the shader uses.
2. **Islands, style A**: low green islands with a gentle ridge (peak about 0.21 x radius, was 0.38 to 0.56), a long gentle sand beach (see 10) and a coast with bays and headlands inside the 1.08 x radius collision circle (`island-shape.ts`, `island-visual.ts`).
3. **Shore field and surf** (`shore.ts`, `swash.ts`, `water.ts`): the water measures the shore from the real coast curve (a per-island table packed into a texture, built from the same `islandShape` as the land). Surf runs parallel to the coast, breaks 9 to 24 m out (was 4.5 to 12, see 10), a swash sheet runs 3 to 9 m up the sand and back with wet sand. The old flat foam ring is gone. The water mesh and its vertex shader are not displaced near the shore, so boats and the CPU wave height are unchanged.
4. **Colour**: turquoise, clear and varied: Beer-Lambert absorption, a refracted sandy floor with caustics, deep blue to teal to turquoise toward the beaches, wide blue and teal patches in open water (`sea-style.ts`).
5. **Reach of the water** (`water-extent.ts`): the disc is dense to 480 m and reaches 3,000 m, where the fog has hidden it, so a high camera never sees the edge.
6. **Camera** (`chase-camera.ts`): 17.5 m behind and 9.6 m high looking 34 m ahead (was 14.5, 6.8, 30); it keeps clear of islands and piers and looks into turns.
7. **People**: cartoon figures built in code (`npc-look.ts`, `npc-visual.ts`), five looks, wave and walk poses; the three market vendors use them. The Blender GLB people stay in the repository, unused. _Replaced by 13: the code-built figures are deleted._
8. **Blender props** (`tools/blender/props.py`): coconut palms, rocks, a stilt hut and bushes built from the palette into `public/models/*.glb`, placed as instances (`island-models.ts`, `island-layout.ts`), with the procedural props kept as the fallback. The script never calls `lib.reset()`, so it runs inside a running Blender through the Blender MCP.
10. **Long gentle beach** (user feedback: "the sand beach is too short and steep, so the surf does not look real"; `island-shape.ts`, `shore.ts`, `swash.ts`, `water.ts`, `island-layout.ts`, `market.ts`): the sand rises at 0.07 (4 degrees) at the waterline and reaches 1 m about 11 m inland (it was 25 degrees, full height in 5 m); the shelf off it falls 0.07 of the distance (0.75 m at 10 m, 2 m at 25 m; it was 1.7 m at 10 m); the hills fade in from 9 m inland over 14 m or half the radius, so the coast and the hills' steepness (about 0.6) are unchanged and the grass core is smaller. The sea's depth, colour, caustics and foam use the same profile; breakers are low spilling ones that break 9 to 24 m out, each wave at its own distance, with feathering crests and long bores; the swash climbs 3 to 9 m and the wet band follows it. Consequences: a wave trough now bares up to 2 m of the shelf (the drawn bed dips 0.35 m more off the sand, the sea is opaque so only this shows), the water knows its own depth over the sand and draws a thin white edge there; palms, bushes and the hut stand behind the dry sand, the rowboat lies 4 to 7 m up the sand, and each market gangway is now 18.8 m long on piles and lands 8.5 m up the sand, where the beach has risen to its planks. Images only (software WebGL); not tried on a phone.
9. **Reflections, full level** (in progress): a half-resolution planar reflection of the islands, props, markets, boats and sky mirrored in the water, replacing the analytic-sky-only mirror, with the old mirror as the fallback.
11. **Surf in tongues** (user feedback: the breakers hit the beach as one band, which looks fake; `shore.ts`, `swash.ts`; commit 9cf76dd): the along-shore arrival jitter only varied over 15 to 48 m, so a whole beach took each breaker almost together. `surfJitter` gained a third term a few metres wide, and each swash wave gets its own early/late offset keyed to its cycle number. Shader only; the CPU wave height and the boats are untouched. Seen in one still frame per view under software WebGL; the motion was not reviewed.
12. **Cat vendors** (user feedback: the three vendors are three chibi cats dressed like people, not the old figures; `cat-pose.ts`, `cats.ts`, `market-vendor.ts`, `model-loader.ts` `loadPartsModel`): Cô Lan, Chú Tư and Bà Sáu are the GLBs `public/models/cat-*.glb` (five named parts each, pivots at the neck, shoulders and tail base), loaded once per scene and animated by rotating the parts (breathing, head, tail, a wave 2.2 s in every 8). The code-built figures (`npc-look.ts`, `npc-visual.ts`, `tests/npc.test.mjs`) are deleted; `PAL.people` stays because the Blender scripts read it. About 10,400 triangles are added in place of the old figures' 10,500, so the cap is unchanged. Images only (software WebGL); not tried on a phone.
13. **Taller sea and water at the hulls** (user feedback: the water looks flat and solid, the boat does not touch it): waves raised from 0.8 to about 1.4 m summed (long swell more than chop), a collar of white water and rings of ripple around every hull, still or moving (`waves.ts`, `water.ts`; details in `docs/ocean.md`). The white caps, the drawn sea floor and the shallow-water maths were re-tuned for the taller sea so the beach stays clean.

## Consequences and limits

- Cost went up on purpose: about 170,000 triangles (the test cap is 200,000), a heavier water shader (shore lookups for eight islands, Worley foam and caustic cells, two ripple reads) and, with reflections, a second scene pass. The first thing to do in the performance pass: measure on a real phone, move the boats' shadow out of the water shader into a small texture, reduce the shore lookups to the islands near the camera, and lower the reflection resolution.
- Real-phone behaviour, frame rate, texture formats on mobile GPUs and long sessions are unverified.
- Known look issues: the unbroken swell now shows as thin patchy white dashes 15 to 40 m off the beach (feathering; the dashes are not continuous lines), foam holes still slightly regular close up, backdrop islands have surf but no swash, and the swash film's front shows little foam at its highest point. The wider beach leaves less grass: about 11 m of each island's 24 to 36 m of land is dry sand.
- The NPC, camera and waves were checked in a Babylon test scene and in the real-scene browser tests under software WebGL.
