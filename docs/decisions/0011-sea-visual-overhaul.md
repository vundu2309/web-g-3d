# 0011: The sea's visual overhaul (water, islands, people, camera)

Status: accepted (user-directed, 2026-11-13); steps 1 to 4 below are on `main`, reflections and bushes were in progress when this was written.

## Context

The user found the sea flat and repetitive (a grid of ripples, one flat colour, no surf), the islands too pointy to look real, the market people ugly, and the camera too low and close. They asked for the water first, then the rest, with these standing rules: **make it beautiful first, phone performance is refactored later**, the sunset stays fixed, and every visual change is shown as before/after images (software-rendered, never a real phone) and approved before it is pushed. The images are the evidence; nothing here has been run on a real phone and no frame rate has been measured.

## Decisions

1. **Waves and ripples** (`waves.ts`, `ripples.ts`): twelve waves (2.2 to 61 m) spread around `WIND_DIRECTION`; fine ripples from a 128 x 128 slope texture built at start-up and read in two layers, modulated by calm and rough patches and faint wind streaks. The CPU height the boats float on is the same table the shader uses.
2. **Islands, style A**: low green islands with a gentle ridge (peak about 0.21 x radius, was 0.38 to 0.56), a long gentle sand beach (see 10) and a coast with bays and headlands inside the 1.08 x radius collision circle (`island-shape.ts`, `island-visual.ts`).
3. **Shore field and surf** (`shore.ts`, `swash.ts`, `water.ts`): the water measures the shore from the real coast curve (a per-island table packed into a texture, built from the same `islandShape` as the land). Surf runs parallel to the coast, breaks 9 to 24 m out (was 4.5 to 12, see 10), a swash sheet runs 3 to 9 m up the sand and back with wet sand. The old flat foam ring is gone. The water mesh and its vertex shader are not displaced near the shore, so boats and the CPU wave height are unchanged.
4. **Colour**: turquoise, clear and varied: Beer-Lambert absorption, a refracted sandy floor with caustics, deep blue to teal to turquoise toward the beaches, wide blue and teal patches in open water (`sea-style.ts`).
5. **Reach of the water** (`water-extent.ts`): the disc is dense to 480 m and reaches 3,000 m, where the fog has hidden it, so a high camera never sees the edge.
6. **Camera** (`chase-camera.ts`): 17.5 m behind and 9.6 m high looking 34 m ahead (was 14.5, 6.8, 30); it keeps clear of islands and piers and looks into turns.
7. **People**: cartoon figures built in code (`npc-look.ts`, `npc-visual.ts`), five looks, wave and walk poses; the three market vendors use them. The Blender GLB people stay in the repository, unused. _Replaced by 13: the code-built figures are deleted._
8. **Blender props** (`tools/blender/props.py`): coconut palms, rocks, a stilt hut and bushes built from the palette into `public/models/*.glb`, placed as instances (`island-models.ts`, `island-layout.ts`), with the procedural props kept as the fallback. The script never calls `lib.reset()`, so it runs inside a running Blender through the Blender MCP.
9. **Long gentle beach** (user feedback: "the sand beach is too short and steep, so the surf does not look real"; `island-shape.ts`, `shore.ts`, `swash.ts`, `water.ts`, `island-layout.ts`, `market.ts`): the sand rises at 0.07 (4 degrees) at the waterline and reaches 1 m about 11 m inland (it was 25 degrees, full height in 5 m); the shelf off it falls 0.07 of the distance (0.75 m at 10 m, 2 m at 25 m; it was 1.7 m at 10 m); the hills fade in from 9 m inland over 14 m or half the radius, so the coast and the hills' steepness (about 0.6) are unchanged and the grass core is smaller. The sea's depth, colour, caustics and foam use the same profile; breakers are low spilling ones that break 9 to 24 m out, each wave at its own distance, with feathering crests and long bores; the swash climbs 3 to 9 m and the wet band follows it. Consequences: a wave trough now bares up to 2 m of the shelf (the drawn bed dips 0.35 m more off the sand, the sea is opaque so only this shows), the water knows its own depth over the sand and draws a thin white edge there; palms, bushes and the hut stand behind the dry sand, the rowboat lies 4 to 7 m up the sand, and each market gangway is now 18.8 m long on piles and lands 8.5 m up the sand, where the beach has risen to its planks. Images only (software WebGL); not tried on a phone.
10. **Reflections, full level** (in progress): a half-resolution planar reflection of the islands, props, markets, boats and sky mirrored in the water, replacing the analytic-sky-only mirror, with the old mirror as the fallback.
11. **Surf in tongues** (user feedback: the breakers hit the beach as one band, which looks fake; `shore.ts`, `swash.ts`; commit 9cf76dd): the along-shore arrival jitter only varied over 15 to 48 m, so a whole beach took each breaker almost together. `surfJitter` gained a third term a few metres wide, and each swash wave gets its own early/late offset keyed to its cycle number. Shader only; the CPU wave height and the boats are untouched. Seen in one still frame per view under software WebGL; the motion was not reviewed.

12. **Taller sea and water at the hulls** (user feedback: the water looks flat and solid, the boat does not touch it): waves raised from 0.8 to about 1.4 m summed (long swell more than chop), a collar of white water and rings of ripple around every hull, still or moving (`waves.ts`, `water.ts`; details in `docs/ocean.md`). The white caps, the drawn sea floor and the shallow-water maths were re-tuned for the taller sea so the beach stays clean.
13. **Cat vendors** (user feedback: the three vendors are three chibi cats dressed like people, not the old figures; `cat-pose.ts`, `cats.ts`, `market-vendor.ts`, `model-loader.ts` `loadPartsModel`): Cô Lan, Chú Tư and Bà Sáu are the GLBs `public/models/cat-*.glb` (five named parts each, pivots at the neck, shoulders and tail base), loaded once per scene and animated by rotating the parts (breathing, head, tail, a wave 2.2 s in every 8). The code-built figures (`npc-look.ts`, `npc-visual.ts`, `tests/npc.test.mjs`) are deleted; `PAL.people` stays because the Blender scripts read it. About 10,400 triangles are added in place of the old figures' 10,500, so the cap is unchanged. Images only (software WebGL); not tried on a phone.
14. **The Blender boat, a new rod and line, and the cast bug** (user feedback: put the approved Blender boat in the game; make the rod and line cute; "Không ném được cần", the player cannot throw the rod; `boat-model.ts`, `boat-paint.ts`, `rod-plan.ts`, `rod-visual.ts`, `fishing-gear.ts`, `ribbon.ts`, `cast-rules.ts`, `fishing-ui.ts`; tests `boat-model`, `rod-plan`, `cast-rules` and the browser test `ocean-cast`): **Boat**: `public/models/boat.glb` (3,697 triangles, parts hull, deck, canopy, engine, flag, lantern) replaces the code-built boat for every player, loaded once, painted per palette (two roles replaced, one mesh per palette, instances), the lantern a glowing mesh of its own; the code-built boat stays as the synchronous fallback. The file's bow is at glTF +Z and the scene is right-handed (no loader flip), so the model is turned half a turn about Y when loaded (`BOAT_YAW`) to put the bow at −Z with the rod holder at `ROD_BASE`. The Blender boat has no fisherman (the fallback keeps him). The code-built boat measured 9,506 triangles, the model 3,697. **Rod and line**: a 4 m chunky rod (cork handle, ferrule, four hard bands in the boat's own colours, four guides, a round orange tip; one 304-triangle mesh that bends) with a round reel and crank (540 triangles); the line a camera-facing strip about 2 to 3 px wide with a dark halo; the lure a plump coloured fish with a hook that shows only in the water. **Cast bug, root cause**: pressing the fishing button while a landed fish still hung from the rod only kept the fish and returned (`press()` did `settle(true); return;`), so the first press after every catch did nothing (the player holds the button, sees nothing charge, lets go, nothing is thrown) and the doc and the comment said a new throw keeps the fish. A browser test with touch pointer events reproduced it (the keep happened, the phase never became `charging`); now one press keeps the fish and starts the throw (`pressAction`). Also found and fixed: a throw toward an island showed only a button shake (the marker ring lay under the sand and the bar looked normal), so it now shows red (bar, arc, a ring drawn over the land, a red bar that stays about a second); the charge power could be slightly negative on a frame that began before the press; and the fishing gear was built at the first press (a hitch while the button was held), so it is built with the scene. **Not a bug**: the lead's probe saw the phase stay `idle` for a second during a hold and `ocean-fishing`'s "power bar fills while held (100 -> 100)" failed once: both are software WebGL frames of 0.2 to 0.9 s (a frame that takes longer than the 1.4 s charge shows a full bar at its first look; the probe's frame gaps were 383, 300, 900 and 416 ms), the input layer received the press at once with mouse and touch events. That test now holds again (up to three times) when its first look at the bar is already full. Not verified on a real phone.
15. **The wake, second pass** (user feedback with a phone screenshot: blocky white wash, a hard cut, closed outline loops, a black shadow; `hull-water.ts`, `boat-shadow.ts`, `water.ts`, `wake-trail.ts`, `wake-mesh.ts`): the cells, the cut and the loops came from the hull water's stretched-ellipse units, its single noise lattice and its 10 m cut-off, not from the wake strips; those are measured in metres, softened and faded inside a 12 m reach, and the strips got a soft foam texture, strips that end in nothing, arms that fade in a turn and a softer shadow (details in `docs/ocean.md`, "The wake, second pass"). A later user report ("the foam halo is far too thick and big", a round collar 1 to 2 m thick that wrapped the whole hull and ran ahead of the bow) made the collar a thin broken lap: at most 0.9 m from the waterline (about half a metre showing past the planking), a small splash at the stem that stops 0.2 m past the tip, nothing behind the transom beyond 0.15 m (the wake strips take it on), gaps in it, and ring-crest foam cut to 0.35 of its strength and 1.6 m of reach (`COLLAR` in `hull-water.ts`, pinned in `tests/hull-water.test.mjs`). Seen in software-rendered images only.
16. **Blender sea animals** (user: replace the code-built animals with the new cute Blender models; `glb-parts.ts`, `fish-models.ts`, `creature-kit.ts`, `creature-hang.ts`, `app/fish-thumb.ts`, `tests/fish-models.test.mjs`): the 16 body plans and two own-pattern species are `public/models/fish-*.glb` (792 to 1,392 triangles each, 1,459,976 bytes for 18 files; the code-built animals were 2,608 to 5,172 triangles); a pure parser reads them with no WebGL so the scene and the store thumbnails use the same data; four colour roles are replaced with the species' colours at load, the five fixed colours stay; files load lazily and are preloaded after the first frame without blocking `ready()`; until a file arrives, or if it fails (one console warning), the code-built animal is drawn and changes into the model in place; the code-built geometry is kept as the fallback, nothing was deleted. Orientation: no axis is flipped (right-handed scene, nose +X, up +Y); the octopus and seahorse hang by their hook (`creature-hang.ts`), a fish hangs as before. Limits: patterns are baked per plan, so a species looks like its plan (only `ca-he` and `ca-ngu` have their own file); the `Look` pattern, iris and sheen no longer shape a model; a ray, crab, lobster, starfish and flounder stand on edge in the water as before. Images only (software WebGL); not tried on a phone, and the 1.4 MB download was not timed on a mobile link.
17. **The hooked fish fights under the water** (user feedback: "cá giằng co thì phải bơi dưới nước chứ sao lại trên mặt nước", the fish sat on the surface in a ring of ripples with a shadow disc; `underwater.ts`, `underwater-material.ts`, `fishing-gear.ts`, `creature-kit.ts`, `reflection-model.ts`, `reflection.ts`; `tests/underwater.test.mjs`): the fish swims 0.3 to 1.0 m below the waves by mood and is drawn through the water. Technique: option (a) of the brief, without a second pass or a change to the sea: the opaque water mesh is left as it is; the fish is a transparent mesh drawn after it whose vertex shader writes the depth of the point where the view ray meets a plane just above the sea (so the already-drawn water does not hide it, but a hull or an island in front still does), and whose fragment shader discards what is above the real wave height and fades the rest into the water colour by depth. Rejected: turning off the water's depth write (the sea floor of the islands, which the opaque water covers, would then show over it), a later rendering group with the depth cleared (the fish would show through hulls and islands) and a fish proxy inside the water shader (the water shader is another builder's, the cost is paid by every water pixel). The line is cut where it meets the sea and continues under it; small rings and a V of ripples replace the ring round a floating fish and the shadow disc; the underwater meshes are kept out of the planar mirror by their material. `fight.ts`, the moods and the button's `data-*` are untouched; `water.ts` was not edited. Limits: from the chase camera at 20 m or more the fish is small and faint (a surge and the rings are readable, a deep strong fish is a dim shape: that is the brief); a swell between the camera and a fish can still hide it (the depth plane is 0.5 m above the sea under the fish); the sea's wake height is not in the fish's depth; the first fight compiles one shader. Seen in software-rendered images only; not tried on a phone, frame rate unknown.
18. **Clear water: the sea is see-through** (user: make the sea look more like Sea of Thieves, step 1, "translucent water with depth"; `clear-water.ts`, `clear-water-pass.ts`, `water.ts`, `fishing-gear.ts`, `underwater-material.ts`, `babylon-scene.ts`, `render-scale.ts`; tests `tests/clear-water.test.mjs`, a changed line in `tests/reflection.test.mjs`, and the new check in `tests/ocean-babylon-browser.mjs`): the sea stays one opaque shader that writes depth (its mesh, its vertex shader and `waves.ts` are untouched), and what lies under it (hulls, piles, rocks in the shallows, buoy bases, the hooked fish and the line) is seen through it, dimmed and tinted by the water in between (red first, then green) and gone in about 2 to 4 m, with the shallows clearer than the deep. **Decision, how to know what is under a water pixel on a phone** (weighed with numbers): (a) a blended sea over a scene depth texture and a scene colour copy: one more pass over the whole scene (about 380,000 triangles), two full-canvas textures (about 5 MB each at 1688 x 780), MSAA lost or doubled, and sorting against the swash, the wake strips, the foam and the ripples: rejected as too heavy and the riskiest; (b) analytic ghosts in the water shader (the floor already is analytic and stays so): hull signed-distance shapes for up to 12 boats in every water pixel, blobs instead of the real models: rejected for objects; (c) **chosen: a small layer of the under-water things** drawn every frame into a render target three quarters of the canvas in each direction (RGBA8, mips, depth, 4 x multisampled; about 7 MB resolved at 1688 x 780 plus the sample buffers) with a shader that keeps only what is below the shared wave function and writes `colour x sight`, which the water mixes into its body before the Fresnel mirror and the foam (see `docs/ocean.md`, "Clear water"). Measured extra cost: 20,000 to 27,000 triangles in the harness views and 33,509 in the busiest frame of `ocean-babylon` (417,198 triangles with the layer, 383,689 without; the cap of 450,000 is unchanged), one pass, one target, one biased texture read and a `pow` per water pixel; nothing measured on a phone. **Switchable**: `ClearWaterGovernor` switches the layer off after two slow windows (average frame above 52 ms) before the render scale is coarsened (`ScaleGovernor.sample(dt, hold)`), brings it back after eight fast ones at most twice, and `?clearwater=0` keeps it off; off is exactly the old opaque sea, and the hooked fish and the line fall back to the special underwater skin of item 17, which stays in the code for that. Pinned: absorption (red first), the visibility limit, the shallow-to-deep continuity (no jump above 0.02 per 5 cm of water depth), the layer's names, the shader texts (waves.ts used, discard above the highest wave, the instanced vertex path), the water's composition order (body, mirror, foam) and the uniform that turns it off, the governor's windows, the headless scene with the layer on and off. Seen in software-rendered images only (harness, 844 x 390, 932 x 430, 667 x 375); not tried on a phone. Review fix (same day): the first version drew the layer at half the canvas, and the player's keel came out as a saw-tooth line or detached red blocks; it is now 0.75 of the canvas, 4 x multisampled, with a lit thing fading in over its first 0.3 m below the waves and a mip bias of 0.6, and the sight is blended continuously (the first version jumped from 0 to 0.06 at its cut-off and drew a faint outline around shapes over the shelf); `ClearWaterGovernor.set` starts a new measuring window and `setClearWater(on, pin)` pins the state for tests. The fish and the line are a little softer and paler than the old special skin drew them. Visual downsides: a device that cannot multisample gets stairs at the waterline; the layer fades out over the last 3 % of the screen at its borders; the boat's draft is 0.6 m under foam, so the keel is a hint, not a hull; no refraction of position, only a wobble; the layer's depth ignores the wake's height (up to 12 cm).

## Consequences and limits

- Cost went up on purpose: about 170,000 triangles (the test cap is 200,000), a heavier water shader (shore lookups for eight islands, Worley foam and caustic cells, two ripple reads) and, with reflections, a second scene pass. The first thing to do in the performance pass: measure on a real phone, move the boats' shadow out of the water shader into a small texture, reduce the shore lookups to the islands near the camera, and lower the reflection resolution.
- Real-phone behaviour, frame rate, texture formats on mobile GPUs and long sessions are unverified.
- Known look issues: the unbroken swell now shows as thin patchy white dashes 15 to 40 m off the beach (feathering; the dashes are not continuous lines), foam holes still slightly regular close up, backdrop islands have surf but no swash, and the swash film's front shows little foam at its highest point. The wider beach leaves less grass: about 11 m of each island's 24 to 36 m of land is dry sand.
- The NPC, camera and waves were checked in a Babylon test scene and in the real-scene browser tests under software WebGL.
