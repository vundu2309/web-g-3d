# Shared fishing sea

Approved 2026-11-05: each player is a separate fishing boat in one common online map. Enter through the hub's “Ra khơi” (see “Hub, sign-in and loading” below); authenticate with the existing Firebase Email/Password dialog, then sail immediately. No room creation, invitations, lobby, readiness or host. `?view=ocean` restores this direct-entry route after reload. Returning to the hub leaves the sea.

## Implementation and ownership

- `src/app/harbor.ts`: active hub, account observer, entry/exit ownership.
- `src/app/ocean.ts`: controls, fishing interaction, HUD/dialogs, animation and cleanup.
- `src/ocean/model.ts`: deterministic per-UID spawn, bounded movement, island collisions, remote payload validation.
- `src/ocean/babylon-scene.ts`: the Babylon.js scene: one engine, Havok physics for the player's boat, a chase camera, instanced boats for other players and safe text labels. It builds from `water.ts` (wave-displaced sea shader), `sky.ts` (gradient sky, sun, clouds), `boat-visual.ts` and `island-visual.ts` (procedural models merged into one vertex-coloured mesh each, so a boat is one draw call and other players' boats are instances), `havok.ts` (loads the WASM), `render-scale.ts` (resolution governor) and `sea-style.ts` (shared colours and sun).
- `src/platform/ocean.ts`: selects the same configured provider as the account layer. `ocean-firebase.ts` owns the legacy Firestore lease; `ocean-socket.ts` uses the Supabase-authenticated Render Socket.IO API. `server/ocean.mjs` owns the ephemeral shared sea for that API instance.

The original Monopoly board/HUD/artwork and room/auth services remain. `?view=game` is the original local Monopoly preview; `?view=monopoly-hub` runs its preserved room hub. New modules do not use room transitions or alter Monopoly economics/resources.

## Controls and fishing

On touch, one thumb drags the on-screen stick (`src/ocean/stick.ts`). Touching anywhere in the lower-left zone (48% wide, 66% high) makes the ring jump under the thumb and return home on release, so the thumb never has to find it; touching the ring itself keeps it where it is. The title line under “Biển chung” is hidden on short landscape screens, good-news notices (“Đã vào biển chung”, “Đã kết nối lại”) fade after 4 s while still being announced to screen readers, and name labels that would overlap are stacked (`separateLabels` in `src/ocean/indicator.ts`). The stick: up/down is throttle (forward/reverse) and left/right is steering, so a diagonal drag runs and turns at once; farther means stronger, a small dead zone ignores a resting thumb, and releasing or cancelling the touch stops the boat. The other thumb is free: “Thả câu” acts on press because browsers do not synthesize a click for a second finger while the first holds the stick. WASD/arrows still work as full deflection, and the stick wins while held. Fishing is described under “Fishing gameplay” below. Position, the fish store and the catch count are saved in this browser's localStorage (key `photyphu:sea:v1:<uid>`, autosave every 5 s, on each catch or release, when the tab is hidden and on leaving) and resumed on return; a save older than 7 days, outside the sea, inside an island or corrupt falls back to the harbor (the fish store is kept). The save is per device, not per account, and is not a reward, currency or anti-cheat-protected economy.

If the connection drops during play the game reconnects by itself (same boat, same place, up to 90 s, status “Mất kết nối. Đang kết nối lại…”). Revoked sessions, a second window taking over, or a full sea are not retried and show the reason with a way back. Other players see whether a boat is fishing.

The sea is drawn even while connecting or offline; boats appear once connected. `index.html` carries a small classic script: if the game has not drawn after 8 seconds it shows the failure reason with “Tải lại” and “Xóa bộ nhớ đệm và tải lại” (unregisters this app’s service worker and shell caches) instead of a blank page.

Foreground controls fit 844×390, 932×430 and 667×375; portrait falls back to stacked actions. Controls have at least 44px targets, dialog focus, keyboard operation and reduced-motion waves. Native phone performance and OS input behavior still require device verification.

**Look of the controls** (variant A, chosen by the user): icon-only glass buttons (translucent white with a blur; a plain translucent fallback where `backdrop-filter` is missing), all at least 48 px: back (arrow), the fish store (fish icon and the count) and the crew count. The fishing button is the one solid, yellow control (78 px) and its icon shows what it does: a hook (cast), an arrow thrown out (let go), a turning reel (haul in), a jolt (pull now); the words are only its accessible name (`aria-label`: Thả câu / Thả ra! / Thu cần / Giật!, the store button reads “Kho cá n”). There is no first-time hint text. On portrait phones the harbor art is shown wide enough for the whole boat and sun instead of being cropped to the middle (`.hub-art` in `style.css`); a faint seam can still be seen at the bottom edge of the art on a 390×844 screen. Seen in software-rendered screenshots at 844×390, 667×375, 390×844 and 360×640 only, not on a real phone.

## Input layer (no long-press menus)

The sea's controls (back, crew, fish store, the fishing button, the stick ring) are only **drawn**: they have `pointer-events: none`. One transparent layer (`.ocean-input`, `src/ocean/input-layer.ts`) takes every touch, finds the control under the finger by position (hit area at least 44 px even when the drawing is smaller), keeps one route per finger so the stick and the fishing button work together, and blocks `contextmenu`, `selectstart` and `dragstart`. Because a finger never lands on a button, text, icon or link, the browser has nothing to turn into a long-press menu or callout, and nothing that can cancel a held action. A touch the system cancels counts as a lift, so nothing is left held. The buttons stay real `<button>`s for the keyboard and assistive technology: their `click` still works. While the loading card is up the layer is off and the back button takes its own touches. Dialogs (crew list, fish store) are still HTML and are tap-only. This is verified in Chromium with real touch events and a synthetic `contextmenu`; it has not been tried on a real phone or in Safari.

## Fishing gameplay

Rules are pure code in `src/ocean/fishing.ts` (species, zones, the cast, the fish's behaviour, the store); the screen is `src/app/fishing-ui.ts`; the line, the rod's bend, the fish and the ripples are `src/ocean/fishing-gear.ts` (drawn through `OceanScene.setGear`). Every fish state is a signal in motion, not text.

1. **Charge and throw.** Hold “Thả câu” (or Space) and a power bar fills in 1.4 s; let go to throw. The lure lands 8 to 40 m ahead and a little to starboard (where the rod points). A dotted arc and a ring on the water preview the landing; green means the sweet spot (22 to 30 m), where fish wait less and better fish are likelier. A throw that would land on an island or outside the sea fizzles. Keyboard activation without a hold throws at the sweet spot.
2. **Nibbles.** There is no float. The line hangs slack in a curve to the water (a rope, see below). A nibble twitches the line and nods the rod tip, spreads a ring where the line enters the water and shows a blinking “!” above that spot: a fish is about to bite. A round has 1 to 3 nibbles; one fish in five loses interest and another comes later. Pulling now only reels the line in.
3. **The bite.** The line goes taut and trembles, the line is dragged under and away from the boat, the button turns into the pull, a ring around it drains and the screen edge reddens. The pull must come within **1 second** (`HOOK_WINDOW_MS`); after that the fish is gone.
4. **The catch.** The landed fish hangs from the rod tip in 3D (sized by its weight) with a small tag (rarity stars, weight, ✦ for a species record) and two drawn icon buttons: keep (✓, the fish rises away into the store) and put back (the fish falls into the sea). The fish is stored only when kept; throwing again, opening the store or leaving the sea keeps it, so a landed fish is never lost by accident. There is no card or dialog.
5. **Kho cá.** A full-screen hub (not a small dialog) with an X icon at the top right (`Đóng`, also Esc), the count held, and a grid of only the species held now: picture, name, count (`×n`), record weight with rarity stars and a put-back icon button (one fish back in the sea; the record stays). Nothing is shown for species never caught, no silhouettes. The picture of each species is its own 3D model drawn once to a small canvas by `src/app/fish-thumb.ts` (maths in `src/ocean/creature-thumb.ts`: turn, shade, sort far to near; no second WebGL context), so the 2D SVG art is gone.

The line is out (and others see the boat as fishing, the boat is not driven) from the throw until the line is in. A boat that drifts more than 48 m from where the line enters the water, a dialog, or a hidden tab reels it in. Twenty species are in the table (the original ten fish: cơm, bống, chim, nóc, mú, thu đảo, hồng, ngừ, đuối, kiếm; and ten added later: nục, cua biển, sao biển, cá hề, bơn, ngựa, mực ống, bạch tuộc, tôm hùm, mập). Each names one of the sixteen 3D models (`shape`) and one fight style; which can be caught depends on where the bobber lands: near an island shore, in open water, or far from every island (`zoneAt`). Weights are skewed to the light end of each species' range. Everything is simulated on the client with no server authority, so there is no economy, trade or reward; catching and releasing are personal. Not done yet: schools of fish visible on the sea, a fight with line tension, and other players seeing the bobber (they still see the existing short line).

### Tug of war (after the hook)

Pulling inside the one-second window now only **sets the hook** (`hooked`); the fish is landed only after a fight (`src/ocean/fight.ts`, pure rules with their own tests). From then on the button is **held to reel and released to give line**. The fish is in one of three moods: **surge** (it bursts away and takes line: reeling against it spikes the line tension and snaps the line, so let go), **strong** (it pulls steadily and surges now and then: short pulls win line and tire it, reeling too long builds tension until it snaps) and **tired** (it can be reeled in; left alone for a few seconds it recovers). The fish is landed when the distance to the boat runs out (`caught`); a snap, running out the whole line, shaking the hook after 70 s or leaving the sea (`cancel`) is `lost`. Each kind of animal fights in a style (`steady`, `runner`, `heavy`, `jet`, `crawler`, `glider`; the species table picks one) and bigger fish pull harder and last longer. A frame never advances the fight by more than 0.1 s, so a slow phone sees slow motion, not a jump.

Simulated with a bot: a careful player (reel when tired, or when strong and the line is slack) lands every style at light and heavy weights in about 9 to 22 s; holding the button always snaps the line; never reeling never lands the fish. **These numbers are first guesses, not tuned by play**: the constants at the top of `fight.ts` are meant to be adjusted by feel on a real phone. The button exposes `data-phase`, `data-mood`, `data-tension` and `data-distance` for styling and tests (never as text).

**What the fight looks like** (`src/ocean/fight-look.ts` holds the numbers, `fishing-gear.ts` draws them; no text): the line goes white, then yellow, then red as the tension climbs, hums and trembles harder near the snap and sags when the fish tires. The hooked fish is its species' 3D model at the end of the line, `distance` metres from the rod: it thrashes, swings and splashes in a surge, swims steadily when strong and lies on its side when tired. When the line snaps or the fish gets away the loose end whips back to the rod. Checked with software-rendered screenshots only (844×390): the fish is clearly visible in a surge but small and low when tired and far away; the rod does not bend yet and the button has no tension ring yet. Not verified on a real phone.

**Rod, line and the hand** (no float anywhere). The rod (`src/ocean/rod.ts` for the maths, `src/ocean/rod-visual.ts` for the mesh) is a damped spring on a tapered blank with a cork handle, a reel and a bright tip: the pull of the line bends it (more near the tip, the length stays the same), it springs back and rings after a release, and a surging fish shakes it. The tip it reports is where the line starts, so the line and the rod always agree; every boat has a rod, only the player's moves. The line (`src/ocean/rope.ts`) is a Verlet rope pinned between the rod tip and the lure or fish's mouth: slack when waiting (it hangs in a curve and rests on the sea), straight and humming when a fish pulls, and its pull on the rod comes from the fight (a surge is hard, a tired fish hardly pulls). The rope's gravity is scaled down as it tightens, since tension swamps its weight. While a fish pulls the line has no spare length at all (a few per cent of spare line sags the middle by metres: 3 % of 20 m is about 2.5 m), and it is drawn pulled onto the straight chord from the rod tip to the fish's mouth, because the simulation lags a fish that swings fast; waiting, it keeps a gentle sag of about half a metre, and a tired fish lets it curve a little. The drawn line is a flat strip that always faces the camera (`src/ocean/ribbon.ts`), thicker with distance, because a GL line is one pixel wide and vanishes; it is smoothed (`smoothRope`) so a nearly taut rope is not saw-toothed. The browser test samples the drawn line while a fish pulls and fails if its middle hangs more than half a metre off the straight line. Each ripple stays where it was made, so a running fish leaves a trail of rings. Fish are drawn larger the farther they are (about 2.5 times at 25 m) so they can be seen. A landed fish leaps from the water to hang by the mouth just below the rod tip, its length (1.7 to 3.2 m in the scene) following how heavy it is for its species.

**Device vibration** (`src/app/haptics.ts`, pure and tested with a fake `vibrate`): while the fish surges the phone buzzes without a break, with longer pulses as the line tightens; a taut line near the snap ticks as a warning; a tired fish is quiet; it stops when the fish is landed, lost or the player leaves. It needs `navigator.vibrate`: **Safari on iPhone has none, so iPhones feel nothing**; Android (Chrome) is the only place it can be felt, and I have not felt it on a real phone.

## Sea animal models

Every sea animal is built from code, like the boat: plain geometry in `src/ocean/creature-geometry.ts` and `src/ocean/creatures.ts` (no Babylon, so it is tested without a GPU: valid finite arrays, a triangle budget of 6,000 per animal, facing +X, the hook inside the body, closed bodies facing outward), and `src/ocean/creature-kit.ts` turns one into a body mesh plus a tail that swings on its own pivot, with one shared material and the colours carried by the vertices. Sixteen body plans: the eight fish shapes already in the species table (`slim`, `round`, `diamond`, `puffer`, `chunky`, `torpedo`, `flat`, `sword`) and `shark`, `flounder`, `seahorse`, `squid`, `octopus`, `crab`, `lobster`, `starfish`; the species supplies the body and fin colours (and optionally a `Look`: pattern, marking and iris colour). Fish have markings on the skin (lateral stripe, bars, spots, mottling), fins made of rays and membrane, gill covers, a mouth and layered eyes; the other animals have suckers, jointed legs, toothed claws, ribbed segments and tubercles. A ray, crab, lobster and starfish are built to show their backs to a viewer at -Z. Renders: `docs/design/sea-creatures-prototype.png` (all sixteen) and `docs/design/sea-creatures-detail-{A,B,C}.png` (larger), taken with software-rendered Chromium, not a phone. An ink outline (inverted hull) was tried and dropped: it swallowed the thin fins. They are drawn in the game: the hooked fish, the fish hanging from the rod and the store thumbnails all use them. Species balance (first guesses, not tuned by play): shore gives the small common ones (cơm, bống, cua, sao biển, nục) and some rarer (hề, bơn, ngựa, nóc, bạch tuộc, tôm hùm); open water mixes thu, hồng, mú, mực and bơn; deep water gives ngừ, đuối, kiếm, mực, bạch tuộc and the rare mập (60 to 400 kg, `heavy`).

## Map detail

The three islands and the sea around them carry more detail, all scenery: nothing here is solid and nothing is shared state (the boats still only collide with `islands` in `model.ts`, and the server is unchanged).

- **Islands** (`src/ocean/island-visual.ts`, `island-props.ts`, `geo.ts`): a finer terrain grid (56 × 56, was 44 × 44) with patches of dry grass, ripples in the sand, a wet-sand band and layered rock; hundreds of grass tufts and flowers, bushes, broadleaf trees next to the palms (more palms than before), clusters of angular boulders at the shore (some in the shallows), shells and starfish, driftwood, a wooden boardwalk with rails and a lantern down to the beach, a stilted hut with a roof, a rowboat on the sand, and a door, windows and a railing on the lighthouse. The small things are written into one buffer (`Geo`) and merged with the terrain, so each island is still one mesh. Seeded: every client builds the same island. A pier into open water was tried and dropped: the beach is only about a metre wide inside the ring boats cannot enter (1.08 of the radius), so a pier would have been sailed through; the boardwalk ends inside the ring.
- **The sea** (`src/ocean/sea-layout.ts` for the data and the gull flight, `sea-props.ts` for the meshes): eight red and green channel buoys (a gate north of the harbor and a marked channel off each island) that bob and lean with the waves, seven seagulls circling the islands (instances of one small mesh), and five coarse backdrop islands beyond the edge of the sea (`SEA_BOUND`), seen through the haze and unreachable. Buoys are not solid, so a boat can sail through one.
- **Cost.** Measured in a software-rendered browser: the active triangles were about 80 000 at the harbor (every island and the backdrop in view), 40 000 to 54 000 at an island, against about 44 000 before. `tests/map-detail.test.mjs` runs headless (no GPU) and holds each island between 9 000 and 26 000 triangles and a backdrop island under 6 500. **The frame rate on a phone has not been measured**; if it is too slow, the grass, flowers and backdrop are what to cut first.
- Seen only in software-rendered screenshots at 844×390.

## Online contract and limits

On the Firebase configuration, all users query the same `monopolyHub/v1/oceanBoats` collection ordered by `updatedAt` descending, limited to 100 most recently updated boats. No silent offline/demo fallback or sample players. Identity is a UID-keyed document; display name contains no account email. A new tab for the same account replaces its old session, which stops controlling the boat. Leave deletes only a matching lease; abrupt loss expires visually within 30 seconds. Hidden tabs stop motion/heartbeats and can expire remotely; foreground entry resumes updates. Deletion is best effort on abrupt tab closure.

Transactions fail offline rather than queue old motion. Motion writes on the Firebase adapter are at most 1 Hz, idle heartbeats at 10 seconds, remote render positions are interpolated. Firestore read/write volume grows with active boats and is unsuitable for a large continuous-action world without an authoritative realtime server. Coordinates and ownership are validated by prototype rules, but movement/fishing remain client-simulated. There is no persistent catch economy, trading, chat, matchmaking or server anti-cheat. Production backend permissions must be deployed separately; the frontend does not grant access by itself.

On the Supabase configuration, the existing account and password-recovery flow are preserved. All sea clients enter the `ocean:shared` Socket.IO channel on the existing API; user identity comes from server-validated Auth, never a payload UID. Joining validates the token once, enforces one active session per UID, validates finite bounded pose fields, bounds payload/rate and caps the sea at 100 boats. Joined sea clients alone receive pushes. Disconnect removes the matching boat; old replaced sockets cannot remove the new boat. No polling or Supabase schema writes are introduced.

### Transport (protocol 2)

`server/ocean.mjs` relays; clients still simulate their own boat. Measured with 50 simulated boats it cut one client's inbound traffic from about 350 KB/s to 6.6 KB/s while updates became five times as frequent, and moves stopped calling Supabase (about 44 calls/s before).

- **Moves are synchronous.** `ocean:move` (client to server, no acknowledgement, volatile) is an in-memory write, validated and rate limited (100 ms per socket). Too-fast or invalid moves are dropped silently; losing the lease is reported with `ocean:error`.
- **One tick, deltas only.** A fixed tick (200 ms, only when something changed) sends `ocean:delta` `{t, add:[{uid,name,x,z,heading,fishing,updatedAt}], up:[[uid,x,z,heading,fishing 0/1,movedAt]], gone:[uid]}`. A joiner first gets one full `ocean:state`. Entering and leaving are flushed at once.
- **Session checks on a timer.** The session is re-validated every 60 s per sea socket (`authRecheckMs`). A rejected token ends the connection with `ocean:error` "Hãy đăng nhập lại."; the Auth provider being unreachable never does (`authenticate` throws on 5xx, returns null only on 401/403). The client sends a renewed access token on the open connection with `ocean:token`, so a session is not cut when the first token (about one hour) expires. If a server without this handler cuts the connection, the client reconnects once with a fresh token; only a refused handshake means signing in again.
- **Playback, not chasing.** Clients keep a short history per remote boat (`src/ocean/remote.ts`) and draw it about 500 ms in the past (2.5 ticks: the sender's period, the wait for the next tick and jitter), interpolating between samples stamped with when the boat moved, not with the tick that carried them. Simulated with 60 ms of jitter and a sender whose pace drifts against the tick, a 300 ms trail left about a fifth of the frames standing still and 450 ms none (`tests/ocean.test.mjs` pins this); `ServerClock` follows the fastest recent path so jitter does not move playback. The renderer is unchanged.
- **Compatibility.** The client announces `proto: 2` when joining; the server answers with `proto`, `sendMs` and `delayMs`. A server without it keeps the old behaviour (acknowledged `ocean:command` moves, full `ocean:state` snapshots, 1 Hz, 1.3 s playback delay); a client without it keeps getting full snapshots. Deploy order does not matter.
- **Not done:** interest management (sending only nearby boats) and binary encoding; with at most 100 boats at 5 Hz they are not needed yet. The server is still one instance with in-memory state.

This Socket.IO sea is intentionally ephemeral and requires exactly **one API process/instance**: restart/redeploy clears boats and players re-enter; positions and catches are not saved. Do not scale Render to multiple instances until shared authoritative world state/broadcast is implemented. Existing PostgreSQL LISTEN/NOTIFY continues serving the preserved room hub; it is not falsely presented as synchronizing the ephemeral sea across instances. The adapter fails visibly and returns to the hub on connection failure instead of switching providers.

## Boat physics (Babylon.js + Havok)

See [decision 0008](decisions/0008-babylon-havok-boat-physics.md). `src/ocean/boat-physics.ts` runs the hull as a Havok rigid body and adds, as per-step impulses, buoyancy at six hull-bottom points against `src/ocean/waves.ts`, quadratic water drag that is far stronger across the keel than along it, propeller thrust, and a rudder whose authority grows with speed. Islands are static cylinder colliders (`islandCollisionRadius`, just past the visible beach), and a soft wall keeps the boat inside the sea the server accepts. The player's boat is Havok's; the controller reads its pose each frame (clamped to the sea bounds) and publishes `x`, `z`, `heading`, `fishing` exactly as before, so the server contract did not change. Other players' boats are not simulated: they follow published poses and rest on the wave surface under them. Steering is analog, so the touch stick maps straight onto throttle and rudder; while casting the boat gets no thrust and drag brings it to rest.

The world uses right-handed axes (`enableSeaPhysics`), so heading 0 is -Z and a positive heading swings the bow toward +X, matching the movement model and the published poses. Havok steps at a fixed 60 Hz regardless of frame rate, and a frame longer than 100 ms is clamped, so a slow phone sees slow motion rather than a jump.

**The sea surface** (`src/ocean/waves.ts`, `src/ocean/water.ts`). Seven travelling waves with deep-water dispersion, each a sharpened sine (`((1 + sin) / 2)^sharp`, normalised to a zero mean) so crests are narrow and troughs broad like a real sea. The height is still a pure vertical function of (x, z, t), identical on the CPU (buoyancy, remote boats, the fishing line) and in the vertex shader (generated from the same table), and bounded by the summed amplitudes. The slope is analytic (`waveGradient`, and `waveSlope` in GLSL, checked against a central difference in `tests/boat-physics.test.mjs`), with each wave's slope faded out with distance in proportion to its wavelength and skipped once it is zero, so the far sea only pays for the long swell. The fragment shader adds two crossing ripple layers (gone beyond about 130 m), light through the crests, a sun glitter that widens with distance, white caps on steep crests, and water that turns turquoise with a surf line near the three islands (`islands` uniform). Seen only in software-rendered screenshots at 844×390; **the frame cost on a phone has not been measured** (an in-browser software-render timing did not synchronise the GPU and was discarded). Counting instructions only, the new shader is about as costly as the old one where the far-sea skips apply and costlier near the camera.

**Sharpness.** The sea draws at up to 2x the CSS size. `ScaleGovernor` lowers the pixel count only after two consecutive slow windows (ignoring the first 4 s), raises it again after five fast ones, and never reacts to the 30 fps cap itself. An earlier version treated the cap as "slow" and blurred the sea permanently; `tests/ocean.test.mjs` pins that behaviour.

**Cost.** Measured in the browser test (before the map detail below): about 44k triangles and 10 meshes per frame. The Babylon chunk is 1.73 MB (409 KB gzip) and the Havok WASM 2.09 MB (658 KB gzip). Both load only when entering the sea and are excluded from the service-worker precache (`scripts/pwa-build.mjs`): the sea is online-only. Frame rate on real phones has not been measured.

`tests/boat-physics.test.mjs` drives real Havok headlessly (Babylon `NullEngine`, fixed 1/60 s steps) and checks floating on the local surface, bobbing, bounded roll and pitch, gradual acceleration and coasting, turning both ways with limited sideslip, island blocking without tunnelling, the start heading, exact repeatability and the boundary wall. Removing buoyancy, drag or the island colliders makes the matching tests fail. It runs with `tests/support/register-resolve.mjs`, which only teaches Node to resolve Babylon's extensionless deep imports. `tests/ocean-babylon-browser.mjs` runs the real scene in a browser with a fake connection: 2x draw size on a 3x screen, frame cost, labels, floating, driving and turning with one touch, and a second thumb casting while the first holds the stick.

## Hub, sign-in and loading

Three separate screens, so the game is not a web page with a game inside it.

1. **Hub** (`src/app/harbor.ts`). A sunset over drifting layered waves with the game's own fishing boat bobbing on them (`src/ui/hub-art.ts`, drawn as SVG with no image file, and deliberately not a picture of the 3D map: no islands, no lighthouse; the map shows only once it is loaded), the title, one big “Ra khơi” button, a captain's card (name and the fish held on this device, or a line about the shared sea when signed out) and two round 50 px buttons (install, sign in). It fetches nothing of the sea: no Babylon, Havok or models, which load only after “Ra khơi”. The only early network call is the wake-up ping to the API (`wake.ts`). The hub does not show how many boats are at sea because it has no connection to know that; showing a number would need a server endpoint.
2. **Sign-in** (`src/app/account.ts`, class `account-screen`). A full-screen dialog over the same sunset (a still SVG background, not the map) with the form in a card; nothing of the game is behind it. Signing in leads straight to loading.
3. **Loading** (`startOcean`, `.ocean-loading`). An opaque full screen of the same sunset, a boat, three steps (graphics, connection, entry) and a progress bar. The map and the controls are not shown: the stick, the fishing button and the counters are hidden and only the way back stays. The screen is lifted only when the engine, the models and their shaders are ready (`OceanScene.ready()`, bounded at 8 s), the connection is up and the boat is in the sea.

Tests: `tests/harbor-browser.mjs` (touch targets and fit at 844×390, 667×375 and 390×844; no engine, model or WASM request at the hub or on the sign-in screen; the sign-in covers the screen; the loading screen hides map and controls until ready).

## Loading and connecting

Entering the sea shows the loading screen above, with three steps (graphics, connection, entry). Graphics and the network start together, and `src/platform/wake.ts` nudges a sleeping API as soon as the home screen shows, so a free Render instance (30-60 s to wake) wakes while the engine downloads. The old flow gave up after a fixed 15 s and asked the player to go back to the hub.

`src/ocean/connect.ts` retries instead: pauses of 1.5, 2.5 then 4 s, each attempt abandoned after 25 s, a total patience of 100 s, and a "the server is waking up" note after 5 s. A connection that arrives after its attempt timed out is closed rather than leaked. Errors retrying cannot fix (signed out, opened in another window, sea full, not configured) are shown at once. If graphics take over 60 s or connecting runs out of patience, the card becomes a failure message with a **Thử lại** button (reload, which re-enters the sea directly) and the HUD's **Về hub** stays reachable above it. The same card appears if the connection drops mid-game.

Tests: `tests/ocean.test.mjs` pins the retry policy; `tests/ocean-loading-browser.mjs` runs the card in a browser with a flaky, a silent and a fatal fake server. Not measured: how long a real Render cold start takes from a phone, or the real cost of the wake-up ping.

## Finding each other

Players used to start up to 40 m apart facing random directions, so the other boat was usually behind or beside the camera and nothing said where it was: two real accounts started 27.6 m apart with one 86 degrees off the other's bow, and the data was fine ("2 thuyền") while neither could see the other.

- **One harbor.** `spawn()` (`src/ocean/model.ts`) puts everyone in a small area at `HARBOR` (8 slots plus jitter), all heading 0 toward the open sea, so any two players start within about 25 m and in front of each other. The client uses it for its own start pose and announces it about 0.5 s after entering, so others see the real spot. The Render API still assigns its own join pose until it is redeployed from this code; the client's pose wins as soon as it publishes.
- **Arrows.** `src/ocean/indicator.ts` places, for any remote boat that is outside the view, an arrow on a ring inside the screen pointing toward it with their name and distance (`.ocean-boat-label[data-edge]`). Boats in view keep a plain name label, hidden beyond 75 m.
- **Expired sessions.** The server reports why a socket was refused; `AUTH_REQUIRED` now becomes "Phiên đăng nhập đã hết hạn. Hãy đăng nhập lại." (not retried) and the failure card offers **Đăng nhập lại** (sign out, back to the home screen). Before this, a revoked session was retried every 5 s for 100 s.
- **One boat per account.** A second window of the same account takes over and the first is told so; to test with two players on one device use a private window (separate storage), or two accounts on two devices.
- A server "too fast" reply to a pose update no longer ends the session.

Tests: `tests/ocean.test.mjs` (harbor and arrow maths) and `tests/ocean-multiplayer-browser.mjs` (two real clients through the real server, an arrow with distance when out of view, a revoked session, same-account takeover; only Supabase Auth is faked). Replacing the new start with the old random one, or dropping the expired-session handling, makes that test fail.
