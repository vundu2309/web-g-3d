# Upgrade shop (rod, bait, boat tiers)

## Feedback and goal

Question from the user: which gameplay improvement next. The lead's finding: fish turn into coins (about 10 coins a fish near the shore, 47 in open water, 121 far from the beach, from a 40,000-roll simulation of `rollFish` + `priceOf`), but coins buy nothing, so selling has no goal. [Decision 0010](../decisions/0010-server-economy.md) already promised a shop. The user chose to do it, and answered the open questions on 2026-11-19:

- Boat: a new boat of a higher tier gives **more inventory slots** (not speed). Each tier has **its own 3D model**.
- Rod: a new rod of a higher tier gives a **stronger line** (the line is not sold separately).
- Bait: buy other baits to **replace the default bait**, they make **rare fish likelier**.
- Everything is a **permanent tier**, bought once (no consumables).
- Pace: **medium**. Tier 1 about 100 coins (about 5 minutes of shore fishing), the top tier a few hours and open-water or far-from-beach fishing.
- Where: the **shipyard** sells boats only; rod and bait are bought at the **market** (the cats).
- **Three shipyards**, far apart, one shared model (the sea has 8 islands of radius 91 to 257 m and 5 markets).
- 1 slot = 1 fish. Default boat 20 slots, top tier 200 (the same number as the server ledger's `LEDGER_CAP`). 4 tiers of each kind, the first free. Tier 1 of each kind costs about 100 coins.
- Other players see your boat tier. The user asked for everything ("làm hết"), delivered in the order rod and bait, then slots, boat and shipyard, then other players seeing the boat.

## Brief

What to build:

1. **Server foundation.** A migration with a table of owned tiers per account, a price table, and RPCs `economy_buy(kind, tier)` (charges once, only the next tier, safe against a double press) and a bait choice; `economy_state` returns the tiers. Prices and tier data live in `src/ocean/upgrades.ts`, and a test fails if they differ from the migration (as `tests/economy.test.mjs` does for fish). `EconomyApi` and `memoryEconomy` follow the same rules.
2. **Effects.** Rod tier slows how fast the line tension builds in `fight.ts` (the line snaps at `SNAP` = 1; the default tier keeps today's numbers). Bait tier multiplies the weight of rarity 2 and 3 fish in `rollFish`, on top of `SWEET_BOOST` (1.8).
3. **Market.** Rod and bait on the market hub next to selling; buying locks the button while the call runs; choose the bait in use; failures shown as short signals.
4. **Slots.** `canKeep(inventory, slots)`; the Keep button is locked when full (put back or sell); a fish already on the rod when the store is full can only be put back (so leaving the sea or opening the store does not keep it); an old store above the slots is kept and only blocks keeping more; the ledger's `full` error is handled as a full store.
5. **Models.** Three boat tiers (2, 3, 4) and one shipyard, made in Blender. Concept images first (stage 2 gate), then structure, then refine. The two colour roles (hull, trim) must stay so the player's paint still applies.
6. **Boat by tier.** The player's own boat shows its tier's model; Havok physics is unchanged.
7. **Shipyards.** Three, on three far apart islands (proposal: islands 0, 4 and 7), placed like the markets, in `seaObstacles`, with a Build button near them and a hub to buy the next boat.
8. **Other players.** The API reads the tier with an RPC called with the player's own token (no admin key anywhere), keeps it with the boat, sends it as a field added at the end of the boat data (old cached clients must keep working), and re-reads it after a purchase (`ocean:refit`). The client draws other boats by tier.
9. **Record.** Decision 0015, `docs/ocean.md`, this record.

Added on 2026-11-19 at the concept gate (user): **the flag on every boat is the flag of Vietnam** (red field, yellow five-pointed star, ratio 3:2). Its colours are fixed (`PAL.flag` in `src/ocean/art/colors.ts`: red 0xda251d, star 0xffff00, regenerated into `tools/blender/palette.json`) and must never equal the hull or trim role colour, because `boat-paint.ts` repaints exact role colours with the player's paint. Tier 1 (`public/models/boat.glb`) is rebuilt with the new flag when step 5 exports the four boats.

Decision of the user, 2026-11-19, on the hull box: **the collision box grows with the tier** (it stays 7.4 x 2.8 m for tier 1; bigger tiers get a box that matches their hull, tier 4 counting its outrigger floats). Places that read the box today and must follow the tier in step 6: `HULL` in `src/ocean/boat-physics.ts` (the Havok box, 7.4 x 2.8 x 1.4 m, mass 900 kg), `HULL_BOX` in `src/ocean/hull-handling.ts`, `HULL_WATER` and `hullHalfWidth` in `src/ocean/hull-water.ts` (the wake the boat stirs), `BOAT_LENGTH` in `src/ocean/boat-visual.ts` (the procedural fallback) and `ROD_BASE` in `src/ocean/rod.ts` (a fixed rod mount, which floats on a bigger boat). A better boat must NOT change speed or handling (the user chose slots, not speed): mass and drive constants stay as tuned, tier 1 must behave exactly as today, and `tests/boat-physics.test.mjs` pins it.

Added on 2026-11-19 (user, after the market screenshots): **the hub shows each item as a picture of its own 3D model** (like the fish store does with `fish-thumb.ts` / `creature-thumb.ts`), in the game's low-poly style, not photos or flat icons. Chosen: (a) the pictures come from the game's own 3D models; (b) **every rod tier has its own model and the rod in use is the one shown on the boat** (the user: "cần câu cũng phải có mô hình riêng và hiển thị trên tàu loại đang sử dụng"); (c) the four baits are **giun (worm, the free default), tôm (shrimp), cá con (small fish), mực (squid)**, each with its own model used for its picture. The boats of the shipyard hub reuse the four boat GLBs.

- Rods: the rod is built from `src/ocean/rod-plan.ts` (plain numbers: rings, colour bands, guides) and `rod-visual.ts` bends its vertices every frame, so a rod tier is a different PLAN with the same length (the bend simulation `rod.ts` is untouched), not a static GLB. Each tier differs in handle, ferrule, band colours, guide count and reel, and the same mesh draws the hub picture. The rod on the player's boat follows `loadout.rod`; other players see it through step 8 (the tier travels with the boat tier). New look: concept images need the user's approval first.
- Baits: four small Blender models (`tools/blender/baits.py`), pictures only (the bait is not drawn on the hook).
- Order: bait concept now; rod plans after step 6 (step 6 owns `rod.ts` and `rod-visual.ts`); then the thumbnails in the market hub (step 3 follow-up) and in the shipyard hub (step 7).

Performance convention (user, 2026-11-19: "apply optimized to this new part"). `origin/main` now has [docs/performance.md](../performance.md) (the budget, nine patterns, how a cost is reported). It applies to every step below; each builder reads it and reports ready-to-paste "Performance pass" entries (the docs step writes them into `docs/ocean.md`), each new cost must be off-able (a condition, a governor or a probe switch in `src/ocean/probe.ts` and the Settings card) and counted by a test where it can be counted, and no phone reading is claimed without a phone. What it means for each part:

- Boat tiers (steps 5 and 6, 8): the doc's limit "the player's boat with its rod under about 6,000 triangles" is exceeded on purpose by tiers 3 and 4 (5,757 and 7,991 plus a rod of about 850), so each tier gets an explicit pinned limit; other players' boats get a level of detail by apparent size (pattern 1) with coarser copies made at load by `simplify.ts` (pattern 2); one lazily loaded template per tier, instances for the rest (pattern 4); the probe `?tiers=0` draws every boat with the tier-1 model; the bigger hull's cost in the hull-to-water code is stated.
- Shipyards (step 7): three buildings of 7,537 triangles in six meshes each: merge parts into as few draw calls as possible, draw by size with a coarser and a far copy and nothing beyond the fog (like `prop-lod.ts`, the touch cut of 750 m), batched per level (pattern 4), kept out of the lean touch mirror like the props, and a probe `?yards=0`.
- Rods (step 5c): the rod is rebuilt every frame from its plan, so a tier changes only numbers (rings, bands, guides), never the vertex count by much: pin the triangle count of every rod tier; rods of other players are not bent every frame (check what the game does today and keep it).
- Pictures in the hubs (steps 3b, 7): drawn once on a small 2D canvas from the model's triangles (no WebGL context, no render target), cached per item and per size, built when a hub is first opened (not at load), and the number of triangles sorted per picture is pinned by a test; the boat pictures use the lowest level that still looks right.
- The merge: the 23 commits that brought this convention also changed `babylon-scene.ts`, `boat-physics.ts`, `ripple-sim.ts`, `wake-mesh.ts`, `water.ts`, `src/app/ocean.ts` and `tools/render-harness/preview.html`, which this change also edits. The lead merges `origin/main` into the working tree when the running steps finish (a stash and a fast-forward: there is no local commit to rebase), and resolves the conflicts by hand.

Limits that must hold:

- **Ownership is the server's; the effects are not.** Rod and bait effects are applied on the device, because a catch is still a client report (decision 0010). A patched client can ignore them. Only the story "the server decides the catch" fixes that; say so in decision 0015.
- The migration is **written and tested on a throwaway PostgreSQL, not applied** to the live Supabase project without the user's say (AGENTS.md).
- No browser tests are run (the user's standing instruction); interface changes are checked by unit tests of the pure view model and harness images at 844x390, 932x430 and 667x375.
- Beauty first: new cost is logged under "Performance pass" in `docs/ocean.md`; triangles of the new models are measured, not guessed.
- Phone performance is not measured; do not claim it.

Do not touch: `waves.ts` and the boat physics response, `.codex/`, Supabase, Vercel and Render settings, applied migrations in `supabase/migrations/`.

Owners: no two agents edit one file at once. `src/ocean/babylon-scene.ts` is touched by steps 6, 7 and 8, so those run one after another.

## Review

`sea-visual-reviewer` (read-only, 2026-11-20, whole change; it ran format, tsc, lint, `test:unit` 528/528, `test:api` and `tests/economy-postgres.sh` on a throwaway PostgreSQL 17, which printed `economy ok`). Verdict: changes needed. Findings and what was done:

1. **A failed or slow read at `ocean:refit` demoted a real tier to the first boat for every player** (reproduced). Fixed: `readFit` answers `undefined` on failure, entering maps it to the first boat and rod, a refit keeps what the boat shows; tested (`tests/ocean-socket.test.mjs`).
2. **`click()` (keyboard or assistive) threw a new cast with a full store while a fish hung on the rod, and the fish was then overwritten and lost.** Fixed: `click()` refuses like `press()` does.
3. **The two-second refit limit dropped a second purchase for good.** Fixed: a refit asked inside the gap is done once when it is over; tested.
4. **A failed first `economy.state()` left the whole session on the free loadout.** Fixed: retried at 2, 6 and 15 s.
5. **A store capped at 20 with no way to sell when there is no server economy.** Fixed: no cap without an economy (the header then shows the count only).
6. **Joining awaited the loadout read between the capacity check and the replacement of an older session.** Fixed: the read comes first, nothing awaits between the checks and `boats.set`.
7. Rebuilding the player's rod when the first loadout answer lands mid-fight resets its bend: left (low; only when the first read is slow).
8. A flagship refit against an island ring or a bridge: not checked by anyone (needs play).
9. `pointsOf` allocates a few small objects per call for a hull with no points: upstream already did; left.
10. A refit republishes the fleet with the old server time: not checked against the playback buffer.

Docs the reviewer found wrong were corrected (the refit rule, the RPC count per refit, the fish on the rod being screen state, the yard model's fetch and the pictures' second fetch, the test count). Still weak, said by the reviewer and not fixed: `loadoutOf` in `server/app.mjs` has no test of its own (every socket test injects a fake), the client `ocean:refit` handler has no test, and the DOM logic of `market-ui.ts` and `fishing-ui.ts` has no test (no DOM test infrastructure; the browser tests are off). Not checked by anyone: a browser, a phone, a real Supabase, a real two-client run.
